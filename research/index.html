Symmetries

Symmetries are one of the crucial ingredients of modern physical theories. They can be defined as those transformations of the variables of a given theory that do not change the form of its laws. In my works I focus on the relationship between symmetries understood in this way and the physical equivalence of models related by these symmetries. In "Arguments from scientific practice in the debate about the physical equivalence of symmetry-related models" I argue that recently proposed counterexamples to the principle that symmetry-related models are physically equivalent are not viable if we interpret these models as representing entire possible worlds (and not their parts). In "Motivationalism vs. interpretationalism about symmetries: some options overlooked in the debate about the relationship between symmetries and physical equivalence" I defend this principle from the challenge by motivationalism, according to which symmetry-related models should be regarded as physically inequivalent until a perspicuous account of their shared ontology is found. The topic of symmetries also appears in "The Unmeasurability of Absolute Velocities from the Point of View of Epistemological Internalism", where an internalist concept of measurement is developed in order to shed light on the relationship between the unmeasurability of absolute velocities and empirical equivalence of boost-related models of classical mechanics.

Philosophy of Quantum Mechanics

In my MSc in Physics I have studied various properties that may by possessed by quantum states, which are believed to be specifically “quantum”, together with their “absolute” counterparts (in the following sense: a given state has a given property absolutely if after an arbitrary unitary transformation it still possesses it). The results of this research, together with the discussion of their philosophical relevance, have been published in “Quantumness of States and Unitary Operations”.

Laws of nature

In the philosophical debate about laws of nature the main division is between Humean approaches, according to which laws are merely effective systematisations of particular matters of fact and do not have any independent reality, and a variety of anti-Humean approaches, which attribute to laws reality that is independent of the fact governed by them.
